Serenity Prayer

God, grant me the serenity to accept the things I cannot change, the courage to change the things I can, and the wisdom to know the difference. Living one day at a time, enjoying one moment at a time, accepting hardship as the pathway to peace, taking, as Jesus did, this sinful world as it is, not as I would have it, trusting that He will make all things right if I surrender to His will, that I may be reasonably happy in this life, and supremely happy with Him forever in the next. Amen.

The Twelve Steps (AA)

1. We admitted we were powerless over alcohol — that our lives had become unmanageable. 2. Came to believe that a Power greater than ourselves could restore us to sanity. 3. Made a decision to turn our will and our lives over to the care of God as we understood Him. 4. Made a searching and fearless moral inventory of ourselves. 5. Admitted to God, to ourselves, and to another human being the exact nature of our wrongs. 6. Were entirely ready to have God remove all these defects of character. 7. Humbly asked Him to remove our shortcomings. 8. Made a list of all persons we had harmed, and became willing to make amends to them all. 9. Made direct amends to such people wherever possible, except when to do so would injure them or others. 10. Continued to take personal inventory and when we were wrong promptly admitted it. 11. Sought through prayer and meditation to improve our conscious contact with God as we understood Him, praying only for knowledge of His will for us and the power to carry that out. 12. Having had a spiritual awakening as the result of these steps, we tried to carry this message to alcoholics, and to practice these principles in all our affairs.

NA uses a substance-neutral version of the same Twelve Steps, replacing "alcohol" with "our addiction" — wording available at na.org.
The Twelve Steps of NA (paraphrased)

1. Admitting powerlessness over addiction and unmanageability. 2. Coming to believe a Power greater than ourselves can restore sanity. 3. Deciding to turn our will and lives over to the care of God as we understand Him. 4. Making a searching and fearless moral inventory of ourselves. 5. Admitting to God, ourselves, and another person the exact nature of our wrongs. 6. Becoming entirely ready to have God remove these defects of character. 7. Humbly asking Him to remove our shortcomings. 8. Making a list of all persons harmed and becoming willing to make amends. 9. Making direct amends wherever possible, except when it would cause harm. 10. Continuing to take personal inventory and promptly admitting when wrong. 11. Seeking through prayer and meditation to improve conscious contact with God, praying for knowledge of His will and the power to carry it out. 12. Having had a spiritual awakening, carrying this message to addicts and practicing these principles in all affairs.

Paraphrased summary — official NA wording at na.org
The Twelve Traditions of AA (paraphrased)

1. Common welfare comes first; personal recovery depends on AA unity. 2. One ultimate authority — a loving God as expressed in our group conscience. Leaders are trusted servants, not governors. 3. The only requirement for membership is a desire to stop drinking. 4. Each group is autonomous, except in matters affecting other groups or AA as a whole. 5. Each group has one primary purpose — to carry its message to the alcoholic who still suffers. 6. An AA group never endorses, finances, or lends the AA name to any related facility or outside enterprise. 7. Every group should be fully self-supporting, declining outside contributions. 8. AA remains forever nonprofessional, though service centers may employ special workers. 9. AA as such ought never be organized, but we may create service boards directly responsible to those they serve. 10. AA has no opinion on outside issues; the name should never be drawn into public controversy. 11. Public relations are based on attraction rather than promotion; we maintain personal anonymity at the level of press, radio, and films. 12. Anonymity is the spiritual foundation of all our traditions, ever reminding us to place principles before personalities.

The Twelve Concepts for NA Service (paraphrased)

1. To carry out our primary purpose, NA groups band together and create a service structure that builds, coordinates, and keeps up services for NA as a whole. 2. In the end, NA services answer to the NA groups — the final responsibility and authority are theirs. 3. The groups hand the service structure the authority it needs to carry out the work assigned to it. 4. Good leadership matters in NA; leadership qualities should weigh carefully when trusted servants are chosen. 5. Every job given to the service structure should have one clearly named point where decisions are made and someone answers for them. 6. Group conscience is the spiritual way we invite a loving God to guide the choices we make. 7. Everyone serving on a service body shares responsibility for what it decides and should have a full part in deciding. 8. Our service structure stands or falls on communication that is honest and works. 9. Every level of the service structure must take care to hear and weigh all viewpoints before deciding. 10. Any member of a service body may ask that body to make right a personal grievance, without fear of being punished for asking. 11. NA money exists to further our primary purpose, and it must be looked after responsibly. 12. Because NA is spiritual in nature, its structure stays a structure of service — it never becomes a government.

Prayer of St. Francis

Lord, make me an instrument of your peace. Where there is hatred, let me sow love; where there is injury, pardon; where there is doubt, faith; where there is despair, hope; where there is darkness, light; where there is sadness, joy. For it is in giving that we receive; it is in pardoning that we are pardoned; it is in dying that we are born to eternal life.

Alcoholics Anonymous — the Original Manuscript (multilith edition, January 1939)
by Bill W. and the first members of Alcoholics Anonymous (1939)
The Big Book as it first went out — the master working copy the published book was printed from, with the wording that changed before publication marked.
Public domain (U.S.): distributed in January 1939 with no copyright notice and no registration; under the Copyright Act of 1909 the notice was mandatory on every published copy, so the work has been public domain from the start.
Open / download the PDF — 959 KB
The Varieties of Religious Experience
by William James (1902)
William James's Gifford Lectures on conversion and spiritual experience — the book Ebby T. gave Bill W. in 1934; James's account stands directly behind the Big Book's Appendix II, "Spiritual Experience."
Public domain (U.S.): first published in 1902 by Longmans, Green & Co.; U.S. copyright in works published in 1902 expired long ago.

Maps with no signal
The meeting finder and its whole list live on this phone and work with no signal. Directions are different — they come from a map app, not from this app.
On any meeting, tap Map / Directions (or the address itself) and the address opens in your map app — Google Maps, OsmAnd, or Organic Maps. This app sends nothing; your map app takes it from there.
To make directions work with no signal: while you have internet, open your map app once and download North Carolina. In Google Maps: tap your profile picture, then “Offline maps”, then “Select your own map”. In OsmAnd or Organic Maps: download the North Carolina map from the app’s map list. After that one download, directions to any meeting address work even in airplane mode.
The honest limit: this app does not carry its own street map — street-map pictures for a whole state are far bigger than the app itself. The meeting list is offline; the street map rides in your map app, downloaded once.
